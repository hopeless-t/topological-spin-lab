# EXP-004 — Clean spin-polarized domain-wall transport contract

## Status

**SCIENTIFIC CONTRACT DRAFT / IMPLEMENTATION NOT AUTHORIZED**

This document defines the minimum experiment that must survive review before any
EXP-004 transport implementation is accepted into the trusted physics core.

## Minimum physical question

Can the Wilson-regularized surface-Dirac lattice selected by VAL-001 reproduce
the **clean two-terminal propagation** of the EXP-003 mass-domain-wall mode,
including its direction and spin polarization, without introducing extra
low-energy transport channels?

This is deliberately narrower than:

- a quantum point junction;
- disorder robustness;
- a material-specific NdBi device;
- an AFM-TI symmetry model;
- a conserved spin-current calculation.

Those are later questions.

## Geometry candidate

### Transport direction

Transport is along `y`.

### Transverse direction

Use a **periodic transverse x direction with two well-separated mass sign
changes**.

Reason:

A single chiral channel cannot be treated as an isolated finite-lattice
two-terminal lead without a compensating channel somewhere in the complete
lattice problem. A periodic transverse geometry makes that compensation
explicit as a second, spatially separated domain-wall channel instead of hiding
it at an open numerical boundary.

The two walls have opposite orientation and therefore opposite group velocity.

The separation must be large compared with `xi` and must be convergence
tested.

### Clean baseline

The leads and central region use the same clean Hamiltonian and mass texture.

This intentionally simple baseline asks whether the transport machinery,
Wilson lattice, mode classification, and EXP-003 known answer are mutually
consistent before any scatterer or disorder is introduced.

A perfectly clean baseline is a **known-answer transport calibration**, not a
claim that a physical device has zero backscattering.

## Hamiltonian scope

The physical low-energy target remains

```text
H = alpha (kx sigma_y - ky sigma_x) + m(x) sigma_z
```

with the Wilson lattice regulator inherited from VAL-001.

Canonical generic parameters remain:

```text
alpha = 1.0 eV·Å
m0    = 0.020 eV
w     = 50 Å
xi    = 50 Å
Wilson r = 0.5
```

No parameter is material-fitted.

The exact periodic two-wall mass profile must be written into the final spec
and independently checked to reproduce the local EXP-003 wall slope/profile in
the large-separation limit.

## Primary observables

### 1. Scattering unitarity

Record the scattering matrix and

```text
||S†S - I||
```

as a numerical invariant.

### 2. Two-terminal transmission and reflection

For energies inside the asymptotic magnetic gap and inside the one-forward-
channel transport window, record:

```text
T_LR(E)
R_LL(E)
propagating mode count
```

Landauer conductance may be reported as

```text
G(E) = (e^2 / h) T_LR(E)
```

only with the channel-count and lead convention attached.

### 3. Spatial channel identity

Each propagating low-energy mode must carry a transverse probability profile.

The target forward mode must localize at the expected wall; the compensating
counterpropagating mode must localize at the opposite wall.

A transmission number without spatial mode identity is insufficient.

### 4. Spin polarization of flux-carrying modes

Record

```text
<sigma_x>, <sigma_y>, <sigma_z>
```

for normalized propagating lead modes.

For the canonical wall, the target low-energy mode should approach the EXP-003
known answer `<sigma_x> = -1` as discretization and wall separation converge.

EXP-004 must call this a **mode spin expectation / polarization**.

It must not call it a conserved spin current.

## Known-answer expectations

For a sufficiently resolved, clean, identical lead/device geometry:

- the low-energy target channel exists inside the magnetic gap;
- its group velocity sign matches the EXP-003 wall orientation;
- its spin approaches the EXP-003 `sigma_x` eigenstate;
- the opposite wall carries the compensating opposite-moving channel;
- the clean scattering matrix is unitary within numerical tolerance;
- reflection approaches zero;
- source-to-drain transmission approaches one for the single forward channel;
- no additional low-energy propagating channel appears from a fermion doubler.

These are candidate acceptance targets. Numerical tolerances are not frozen
until a convergence pilot measures achievable errors.

## Required convergence axes

At minimum vary:

- transverse lattice spacing / resolution;
- wall separation in units of `xi`;
- transverse circumference;
- energy sampling within the gap;
- Wilson parameter around the frozen `r = 0.5` reference only as a validation
  sensitivity lane, not as a new optimization search.

The experiment must distinguish:

```text
physical convergence
numerical convergence
finite-size wall-wall hybridization
solver tolerance
```

## Red Team controls

The implementation is incomplete unless it can fail on intentionally broken
cases.

Required candidate controls:

1. remove or disable the Wilson regularization and verify that extra
   low-energy channel structure is detected rather than silently accepted;
2. reduce wall separation until hybridization is observable and verify the
   convergence gate fails;
3. reverse the wall orientations and verify propagation/spin assignment
   reverses consistently;
4. corrupt lead/device matching and verify nonzero reflection is detected;
5. perturb the S matrix or solver output in a unit test and verify the
   unitarity gate catches it.

## Backend boundary

The Scientific Contract does **not** make a transport package part of the
physics claim.

Current implementation candidates:

- Kwant scattering matrix / mode machinery;
- a repository-owned transport solver with an independent oracle.

The first candidate is preferred for a qualification spike because it is a
mature open-source transport package and avoids inventing a scattering
framework before one is needed.

Backend selection still requires an installation/runtime/reproducibility
qualification on the public GitHub Actions runner.

## What EXP-004 may claim if it passes

Only:

> In the frozen generic Wilson-regularized surface-Dirac model and clean
> two-terminal geometry, the separated mass-domain-wall channel reproduces the
> expected low-energy chiral transmission and mode spin polarization within
> explicit numerical convergence criteria.

## Pseudo-Council convergence — 2026-09-25

### Round 1

**Continuum / analytic:** Begin with a transport known answer directly tied to
EXP-003. Do not jump to junctions or disorder.

**Lattice / regulator:** Use Wilson `r=0.5` because VAL-001 already earned it
as the conventional-lattice candidate. Do not reopen regulator selection.

**Transport:** A two-terminal scattering problem is the smallest direct
Landauer experiment. Keep the clean device identical to the leads first.

**Topology / boundary:** Make the compensating channel explicit. Prefer a
periodic transverse double-wall geometry over pretending one chiral channel can
exist alone in a finite lattice lead.

**Spin:** Measure propagating-mode spin expectation. Do not label it conserved
spin current.

**Reproducibility:** Keep the backend outside the claim and qualify it
separately on GitHub-hosted CI.

Open disagreement: whether the first experiment should use a custom solver to
avoid a new dependency.

### Round 2

**Numerical methods:** A custom lead/self-energy/scattering implementation
creates a larger unverified numerical surface than the physics question needs.

**Reproducibility:** A mature external solver is acceptable if exact version,
runtime, Hamiltonian construction, and independent physical checks are frozen.

**Minimalism:** Dependency minimization is subordinate to avoiding a bespoke
transport engine when transport itself is the new capability under test.

- clean two-terminal Wilson + Kwant;

Criteria:

transport/future-heavy, and spin-clarity-heavy weight scenarios, the
two-terminal Wilson + Kwant route remained the modal next-step candidate
(~84–89% of draws). The custom-solver route was the only material competitor
(~11–16%).
