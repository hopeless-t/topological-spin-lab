# EXP-003 — Continuum mass-domain-wall reference

## Status

**Implemented; scientific contract active.**

EXP-003 introduces spatial structure without introducing a lattice regulator.

## Why continuum first

A naive lattice discretization of a single Dirac cone can create spurious
fermion species. The first boundary/domain experiment therefore uses a
continuum model with a known analytic bound state. Lattice regularization,
Wilson terms, staggered-grid methods, and Kwant transport are deferred until a
continuum reference exists.

This is also a scientific-boundary decision: a generic sign-changing Dirac
mass is not automatically an antiferromagnetic topological-insulator domain
wall. Real AFM-TI domain and step channels depend on crystal, magnetic, and
surface symmetry structure that is outside this experiment.

## Hamiltonian

With translation symmetry along y,

$$
H =
-i\alpha\sigma_y\partial_x
-\alpha k_y\sigma_x
+m(x)\sigma_z,
$$

with

$$
m(x)=m_0\tanh(x/w)
$$

for the canonical negative-to-positive wall.

Canonical generic parameters:

~~~text
alpha = 1.0 eV·Å
m0    = 0.020 eV
w     = 50 Å
xi    = alpha / m0 = 50 Å
w/xi  = 1
~~~

These are generic reference parameters and are not fitted to NdBi or another
material.

## Known-answer bound mode

For the canonical wall orientation, the normalizable mode has

~~~text
<sigma_x> = -1
<sigma_y> =  0
<sigma_z> =  0
~~~

and the chiral dispersion

$$
E(k_y)=+\alpha k_y.
$$

The transverse envelope is proportional to

$$
\mathrm{sech}(x/w)^{m_0w/\alpha}.
$$

Reversing the wall reverses both the sigma-x eigenvalue and the propagation
direction.

## Monte Carlo design support

Monte Carlo was used here only to choose an efficient **research method and
sampling design**. It is not scientific evidence for the domain-wall physics.

A 200,000-draw decision analysis varied uncertain weights over correctness,
information gain, numerical-artifact resistance, implementation cost, reuse,
and literature alignment. The continuum known-answer route was selected in
about 97% of draws, well ahead of immediate Wilson-lattice, staggered-grid, or
Kwant implementations.

A separate randomized sampling study varied the dimensionless wall-width ratio
w/xi over 0.25–4. For a profile window of ±12 xi, the largest omitted
probability encountered was about 2.6e-9. A spacing of 0.1 xi was already
sufficient for the finite-window normalization target at that scale. The
canonical profile therefore uses 241 points over ±12 xi.

These Monte Carlo studies are engineering decision support and must not be
cited as measured or material-specific results.

## Acceptance checks

EXP-003 verifies:

- the analytic candidate state has a small residual under the continuum
  domain-wall Hamiltonian;
- the finite ±12 xi numerical window reproduces infinite-space normalization;
- the probability density is symmetric and peaks at the wall center;
- the mass vanishes at the wall center;
- numerical energy expectation follows the analytic linear dispersion;
- the bound-mode energy stays inside the asymptotic massive continuum;
- the spin vector matches the analytic sigma-x eigenstate;
- reversing the wall reverses dispersion and spin as expected.

## Scientific boundary

EXP-003 may claim only that the generic continuum sign-changing Dirac-mass
model reproduces its known localized chiral bound mode.

It does **not** establish:

- an NdBi domain wall;
- an AFM-TI classification;
- a lattice bulk-boundary invariant;
- a Chern number;
- a QAH or axion-insulator state;
- immunity to arbitrary disorder;
- quantized transport;
- absence of lattice-doubling artifacts in a future lattice implementation.

## Literature lineage

- Jackiw and Rebbi (1976) is retained as the historical zero-mode source; the
  original paper is not a topological-insulator paper.
- Nielsen and Ninomiya (1981) motivates caution around naive lattice
  regularization of chiral fermions.
- Mong, Essin, and Moore (2010) motivates the later AFM boundary/step problem
  but requires more symmetry structure than this model contains.
- Zhang, Kane, and Mele (2013), and Hammer and Pötz (2013), provide
  topological-insulator surface/domain-wall context.
- Varnava et al. (2021) explicitly model chiral domain-wall and step channels
  on an AFM-TI surface.
